Data Centers.
The Real Numbers.
The real reason we need them.

And the real consequence of not having them. Florida already waters farms, golf, lawns, condos and licensed cannabis grows at a scale that dwarfs a modern dry-cooled data hall. Compute is not a hobby. It is the scarce industrial capacity of this decade.

Without compute, we lose.

This page is not a permission slip for sloppy projects. It is a request to stop treating data halls as a unique environmental crime while Florida already runs much larger water and land systems in plain sight.

2026 flow
~$800B / yr

PwC’s Global Data Centre Outlook (2 Sep 2026) puts annual data-center capex at roughly $800 billion in 2026, rising toward $1.8 trillion a year by 2050 in the central case.

Through 2050
$31.6T

Same study: $31.6 trillion of global data-center capex through 2050, upside near $50 trillion. The United States is modeled at 48% — $15.1 trillion.

This year, AI only
~$1.0T

Goldman Sachs Research (Aug 2026): about $1.02 trillion of global AI-related capex in 2026, including about $581 billion inside the United States.

Do not quote $20 trillion as money already in the ground. That figure is not a verified current installed-base total. The sourced picture is hundreds of billions this year, trillions over the next two decades, and nearly half of the long-run PwC case landing in the United States — if the country actually builds.

If the United States does not host that capacity, the work, the models, the industry, and the military advantage go somewhere else. Compute is the factory floor of AI.

Water: the scale people leave out

Million gallons per day. Categories are not identical. Read the method notes before quoting.

What the chart actually says

  • Agriculture: about 2.1 billion gallons of freshwater withdrawn daily in USGS Florida 2015.
  • Reclaimed residential irrigation: 303.7 MGD in DEP’s 2024 reuse inventory, serving 591,834 residences.
  • Reclaimed golf irrigation: 123.9 MGD on 519 golf courses and 66,970 reported acres.
  • Closed-loop data center: dry coolers reject heat to air. No evaporative tower, no continuous makeup.
Perspective: documented reclaimed golf irrigation alone is about 124 million gallons per day — before freshwater golf wells and before 304 MGD of reclaimed water going to residential lawns.

The engineering matters

“Closed loop” must mean the right thing.

Specify this

  • Direct-to-chip liquid cooling
  • Sealed recirculating coolant loop
  • Dry coolers that reject heat to outside air
  • No evaporative cooling tower
  • No continuous cooling-water makeup
  • Meter and publicly report remaining domestic water use

Do not confuse it with this

  • A “closed” internal loop tied to an evaporative tower
  • Cooling-tower evaporation
  • Regular blowdown and chemical treatment
  • Continuous replacement water
  • Unreported peak-day withdrawals
  • Marketing language with no binding design standard
The honest argument is not that data centers have no impact. It is that design determines impact, that Florida already accepted much larger water and land systems for lawns, golf, farms and licensed grows, and that refusing compute is how a country loses the next industrial stack.

Why some numbers say “approximately”

Store networks change weekly. The 123 data-center figure is operational records on DC Map’s August 2026 Florida file.

Why there is no fake statewide land total

Florida does not publish audited parcel acreage for every chain or MMTC campus here. Location counts are shown directly.

Why cannabis water is not a single bar

OMMU publishes patients, licenses and dispensations. It does not publish a statewide gallon total for cultivation.

Why $20 trillion is not on the hero stat

PwC’s sourced long-run figure is $31.6 trillion through 2050, not a current installed-cost stock. Goldman’s sourced near-term figure is about $1 trillion of AI-related capex in 2026.

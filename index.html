Leaderboard 02

Water: the scale people leave out

Million gallons per day (MGD). These categories are not identical: agriculture and golf figures are statewide withdrawals; residential landscape irrigation is a transparent estimate; dry-cooled data-center cooling is a design condition.

What the chart actually says

  • Agriculture: roughly 2.1 billion gallons of freshwater withdrawn daily in Florida’s latest complete USGS statewide report.
  • Residential landscapes: a midpoint estimate, not an audited total. UF/IFAS says at least half of household water can go to landscape irrigation.
  • Golf: 187 MGD of freshwater in the cited Florida DEP analysis; some courses also use reclaimed water.
  • Closed-loop data center: dry coolers reject heat to air without evaporation, blowdown or continuous makeup water.
Perspective: Florida golf-course irrigation alone was reported at about 187 million gallons per day. A properly specified closed-loop data center with dry heat rejection can use essentially no day-to-day water for cooling.
The engineering matters

“Closed loop” must mean the right thing.

Specify this

  • Direct-to-chip liquid cooling
  • Sealed recirculating coolant loop
  • Dry coolers that reject heat to outside air
  • No evaporative cooling tower
  • No continuous cooling-water makeup
  • Meter and publicly report remaining domestic water use

Do not confuse it with this

  • A “closed” internal loop connected to an evaporative tower
  • Cooling-tower evaporation
  • Regular blowdown and chemical treatment
  • Continuous replacement water
  • Unreported peak-day withdrawals
  • Marketing language without a binding design standard
The honest argument is not that data centers have no impact. It is that design determines impact—and Florida already accepts much larger land and water footprints for ordinary consumption, landscaping and recreation.

Method, limits and sources

Why some numbers say “approximately”

Store networks change weekly. Commercial directories also count store-within-a-store pharmacies, campuses, buildings and proposed projects differently. The page uses rounded, current planning counts and labels the 123 data-center figure as operational records. It does not pretend that every site has the same acreage, electrical load or water demand.

Why there is no fake statewide land total

Florida does not publish audited parcel acreage for every chain on this page. Applying one assumed acreage to every location would produce a dramatic—but unreliable—number. Location counts are shown directly; land claims should be added only after parcel-level GIS analysis.

Why retail water is not ranked

Chain-specific Florida water totals are generally not public. EPA reports a median supermarket water-use intensity of 31 gallons per square foot per year, but applying that benchmark across mixed formats would be a model, not measured consumption.
